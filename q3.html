Compare and Contrasting: Old Fashioned Typesetting to Desktop Pubishing to HTML and CSS

What's better, and how do they compare?

To be fair, all three of these writing methods are good at specific things. That said, old-fashioned typesetting might be the weakest here. Because of recent advances in technology, old typesetting has largely been phased out. For those of you unaware of what typesetting is, essentially it's the process of hand-selecting letters and symbols and arranging them physically or using a machine on some type of frame that can then be used in a printing press.

Luckily, now we have more advanced technology. The digital era has brought us much easier and less labour-intensive means of sharing and recording information. Desktop publishing is what kicked typesetting out. In the early days, this method lacked accessibility in terms of sizing, but now there is more efficient software. Magazines, marketing teams, books, and more still use desktop publishing.

The most versatile and fluid of these three methods is HTML and CSS. There is no set viewing size; everything has the capability to be altered and made more accessible. In a weird way,, it goes back to old-fashioned typesetting: you need to arrange the tags and content so that, when you click to view your page, it prints properly.
